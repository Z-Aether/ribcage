# The theme

How Ribcage looks, and why, for anyone building a new surface (#58). The values live in `ribcage/static/theme/theme.css`. The visual reference is the **gallery**: with `DEBUG` on, open `/gallery/` on the seeded instance. Directions A (neon orange) and C (peat and bone) were worked out and set aside; they are kept on the local branch `prototype/theme`.

## The feel

The marathons are horror marathons, watched in a dark room. The theme is dark only: there is no light theme. It aims for cosy Halloween, a lantern in a dark kitchen, rather than a party-supply shop: warm aubergine and pumpkin, soft contrast that is easy on the eyes, and playful details such as a bat, a spider and hand-cut corners. It should never look like a generic template, so don't reach for system fonts, gradients over everything, glass panels, emoji icons or the same drop shadow on every card.

## Skins

A page wears the voter skin unless it names admin or run in base.html's `skin` block, which sets `data-skin` on `<body>`. A section may set its own.

- **voter**: rounded, roomy and uncluttered, with bigger values and no hint text. This is where the play lives.
- **admin**: dense, plain ledgers with one row per item. No decoration, no motion and no mist.
  - An act that asks first is a click and then an inline confirm. *Cancel* takes the clicked button's place at its size (`console/cancel.html`), and the button naming the act stands apart, at the start of a line after what the act does. The tab keeps its scroll across the click and Cancel, and from a reset's confirm onto the password it shows, through a `data-keeps-scroll` region round its arms and confirms, so Cancel comes up under the pointer and the password in view (#85). A confirm whose answer can take a while, as a phase move's up to about 20 s of refresh, is disabled once clicked and reads what is under way, *Moving to APPROVE and refreshing trigger data…*, in Pico's faded disabled button, with no spinner (#104). *Approve* on the Films tab asks first once VOTE has opened: it becomes a link that arms a confirm in a row under its own, whose `data-place-of` names the row it keeps the place of, with what it does and costs before the button naming it (#124). An act that asks nothing, as *Drop* on the Films tab, and *Approve* before VOTE has ever opened, is a plain button that reloads the tab where it was, its form naming the page it lands on in `data-lands-on`. Its row, marked `data-keeps-place`, comes back at the same height on screen though the reload closes any open card above it, and the ledger keeps its sideways scroll, so at 390 px the acts stay in view decision after decision; a row the decision takes out of the filter leaves the row after it where it was (#96). The Films tab's runtime box is a small box of minutes on its row's line, its source tag after it, or *required to approve* in the veto's ink when empty, then *⚠ TMDB says 144* in pumpkin while it differs from TMDB's, and a quiet *Save*. A refusal takes a line of its own under the box, in the note box's rose, with the typed value kept and focused (#97).
- **run**: the voter's evening, darker still, because the room is dark.

## Tokens that matter

- **Ground and ink:** ground `#1b1420`, surface `#241b2b`, raised `#2c2134`, lines `#3a2d44` and `#56456a`. Ink `#eadccb` and muted ink `#b8a6b4`, both warm off-whites rather than dimmed text.
- **Accent:** pumpkin `#ef8a3f`, for primary buttons (a link wears one as `a.button`), the wordmark and the pitch. Plum `#9a74c8` frames the open card, and lilac `#c9a6f0` is for links and the focus ring every control shows.
- **State colours**, shared by every later surface:
  - the score scale: sea-glass for wanting it, +3 Top pick `#7dd3c0`, +2 Keen `#4aa292`, +1 Happy `#2e6a62`; grey for 0 Don't mind `#43384b`; dusty rose for −1 Rather not `#dca0b6`;
  - the **veto**: a dashed red border, `#ff6363`, unlike any score;
  - the **trigger icon**: a lime ring, `#b9e46e`, on a tile's frame, with a small lime pill on its bottom-left corner, diagonally across from the veto ribbon and set off the edge the same way (0.5rem up, 0.35rem out), holding the count of the viewer's matched topics the film carries strongly (the rows of the card's matched block that pass the ring's own rule, so a ringed film's count is at least 1), in near-black ink `#1b2a08` (about 12:1 on the pill; the lime itself is about 12:1 on the ground). Lime is the trigger warnings' colour and no other thing's: the text that refers to them wears it as well. The class `.trigger-words` marks *My warnings* in the name menu, the nag bar's link, and *will be marked for you* on *My warnings* and Welcome; the *On your list* label of the card's matched block is lime too, and only that label in the trigger block: the card's DoesTheDogDie links stay in the standard link colour. A link stays underlined on hover and keeps the lilac focus ring (#117);
  - **seen it**: the poster greyed and dimmed. It is the only filter a poster ever gets: every other poster keeps its own colours.
  - The password confirmation's live line borrows two of them, sea-glass when the passwords match and rose when they differ, since no score shows beside it (#72).
- The trigger icon rings a film card's poster or title card too, on a collapsed and an opened card alike, and its count pill follows the ring through every swap that redraws it (#111, #117).
- **Title card fill:** the one muted fill is `#3b2647`, with the title in `#f6d9b8`.
- **Type:**
  - Mystery Quest for display: headings, card titles, the wordmark and title cards. It is bouncy and spooky but legible.
  - Alegreya Sans for body text and labels, chosen for reading.
  - Fraunces italic for **notes**, so the group's own words read as handwritten and warm, apart from the app's voice. A voter writes their own in the same hand, in the note box (#88).
  - Admin uses Alegreya Sans throughout.
- **Shape:** voter corners are hand-cut, no two alike, like a carved pumpkin. The pitch sits under a jagged sliver of pumpkin. Admin corners are small and regular.

## Phase bars

A **phase bar** is the foot of an opened film card, where a voter acts on it, and the one part of the card that changes between surfaces. It sits under a dashed line, in a row of its own beside the poster, and takes the card's whole width on a phone. SUGGEST's and APPROVE's hold the **note box**: a label, *Your pitch* in pumpkin on your own suggestion and *Your note* on the rest, a box with no placeholder or hint, and *Save*. An opened card leaves the voter's own note out of its list, since the box holds it, and a closed card lists it (#88). Its line beside *Save* says *Saved.* or *Cleared.*, or gives a refusal kindly, in the dusty rose the password confirmation borrows. It has no `maxlength`, since a browser cuts a long paste silently; the server refuses it whole instead (#88). On your own card while it can be withdrawn, SUGGEST's bar ends Save's line with a quiet *Withdraw…*. It asks first, as the console's acts do: *Cancel* takes its place at its size, and below come what goes with the film and the pumpkin button naming it, at the start of a line of its own. A withdrawn card fades as it leaves the pool (#85, #89). **VOTE's bar** holds the scale as six pills, the five scores in their colours and the veto in its dashed red, in the `score` component's shape at a 44 px minimum height. The voter's current score is ringed in the ink and the rest sit back. Vetoing fills the veto pill solid red, reading *−2 Vetoed*, dims all five and opens the reason box, the ballot's own *Your reason (optional)* with *Save*. Under them come a *Seen it* checkbox and two quiet read-only lines, *Vetoed by* with each name and its reason in italic parentheses, and *Seen by*, each drawn only when it has someone. A phone gives the pills two to a line at 48 px. On the ballot an opened card is a list item across its list's width, in the tray, a band or the Veto band, and while one is open the tray stops sticking so it cannot cover the card (#122).

## The pool filter

*Everyone's* and *Mine* sit above the pool as two pills, the chosen one in pumpkin and the other quiet, with the choice exposed as `aria-pressed`. They show only once their script has run, so without script the whole pool shows. An empty *Mine* says *You haven't suggested any films.* in the body voice, and the pool's own empty line stays hidden then (#91).

## The poster tile

A **tile** is a poster or its title card with the title and a muted year beneath, in `components/tile.html`; the caller frames it and sets its state classes. The picker's tiles add the original title where it differs, and are buttons that keep the tile's look: reached for, the poster lifts and takes a pumpkin edge. A film already in the pool says *already suggested by …* in pumpkin under its tile, naming its suggester, or *already suggested by you* on the searching voter's own (#92; the dev, 2026-10-23). M6's ballot tiles extend the same tile with the state colours below (#61, #92).

## The ballot

In VOTE the voter's page is the board, full width under the thin header with no side rail. Its header reads *Vote*, then `n of N scored` in the top-pick teal and the standing line in the muted ink (#119). Between them sits the **done block**, in `components/ballot_done.html`: a row holding *I'm done*, `n of N done` in the same bold label type, and the roster, every voter by name in the muted ink, a done voter's name in ink with a top-pick teal ✓ after it. *I'm done* is a 44 px-tall outline button in the muted ink; pressed it fills top-pick teal, with `aria-pressed` set, and it posts the state it sets. The roster is the same for every voter and shows done and nothing finer. The gallery shows the block off and on, its toggles flipping their look and posting nothing (#123).

A **legend** is one line in the muted ink between the header and the board, naming the three states a tile's frame shows: *the lime ring and its number* (in the trigger words' lime) for a topic on your list, a *greyed poster* for a film you've seen, and a *red ribbon* (in the veto's ink) for a film others vetoed. Its wording waits for M10's copy pass (#121).

The **Unsorted tray** is a raised panel with the carved card corners that sticks to the top of the window, holds at most 44% of its height and scrolls inside. Its line is *n left*, in bold, then *counts as "don't mind" until you move it*. The **five bands** sit below in the score order, from +3 down to −1. Each is a row of its score pill, the same `score` component the scale uses, and a dashed area edged in that score's colour, which keeps a minimum height so an empty band is still somewhere to place a tile. On a phone the pill sits above its area. A **ballot tile** is the poster tile with a grab cursor; dragging dims the one in hand. A touch drags only after a short hold, so a swipe still scrolls (#119). The **seen-it toggle** is a small round button with a 👁 on the poster's bottom-right corner, the third corner the frame uses (the veto ribbon is top-right, the count bottom-left), set off the edge the same way (0.5rem up, 0.35rem out). It shows on hover and on keyboard focus of its tile, and stays in view, edged in the ink, while on; where there is no hover it always shows. It sits beside the poster, so the greying never touches it, and pressing it never starts a drag. It is on every tile, in the tray, a band and a veto row, which it greys in the same way; the gallery's toggles flip their tile's look and post nothing (#121).

The **Veto band** sits apart below the five bands, full width, edged in the veto's dashed red `#ff6363` with the `score--veto` pill as its label, *I won't be in the room — a different kind of drop*. A film dropped there is a **veto row**, the tile widened into a raised card with the carved corners: the tile at its start, and beside it (under it on a phone) *counts as −2 for you, everyone can see it* in the veto's ink, the viewer's matched block with its own *Powered by DoesTheDogDie.com* line when the film has a match, and the reason box, the note box's own form with *Your reason (optional)* as its name and the same rose for a refusal. The row drags by its tile; the text and the box beside it don't. Another voter's veto is the red corner ribbon on the tile wherever it sits, one initial per vetoer, with *Vetoed by …* and their names on hover; the viewer's own veto has no ribbon, since its place says so (#120).

## The Suggest a film panel

The panel sits above the pool's filter on a surface of its own: the heading, one *Title* box, and the picker under it, five tiles in a row on a desktop and three to a row on a phone. TMDB finding nothing is one line in muted ink where the tiles would be, and TMDB not answering is the same, with *add it by hand* as a link at its end. Under the tiles one more muted line reads *None of these? Add the year or add it by hand.*, *Add the year* emphasised and not a link, and under the no-results line the by-hand offer stands alone as *Add it by hand.*; either way *add it by hand* is a link that swaps the hand step in, its *Title* box holding what was searched and its *Year* box any trailing year (#92, #94; the dev, 2026-10-23 and 2026-10-24).

The **pitch step** takes the picker's place: the poster beside the title, a muted year, the director and the certificate, then *Your pitch* in pumpkin over a large box in the notes' hand with the pitch's pumpkin edge, autofocused. Under it one quiet line reads *Runtime 144 min TMDB ✎*, whose pencil turns it into the runtime pencil's box of minutes, with no *Save* or *Cancel*, since *Add* takes what it holds. A refusal is a line of its own under its box, in the note box's dusty rose. *Add* is the pumpkin button at the start of the last line, disabled until the pitch and the runtime hold something (the dev, 2026-10-23). On a phone the pitch box and what follows take the step's whole width under the poster. The step for a film by hand has no poster: a title box and an optional year box take its place, and the rest follows the same way, with *Add* waiting on the title too (#93, #94; the dev, 2026-10-23).

## The runtime pencil

Where a voter may correct a film's runtime, the card's runtime line ends in a quiet ✎ in muted ink, which turns pumpkin and tips when reached for. A click turns the line into a small box of minutes with *Save* and a quiet *Cancel*, in place on an open or closed card alike. A refusal takes a line of its own beneath, in the note box's dusty rose, and the typed value stays in the box. Saved, the line shows the new runtime with its *by hand* tag, or *TMDB* again when it is corrected back to TMDB's value (#90).

## The DoesTheDogDie section

An opened film card's DoesTheDogDie section is the card's last row, in a row of its own under the phase bar, so the note box comes before the triggers, and on a phone it takes the card's whole width; a collapsed card shows none of it. On the voter skin it sits on the plain surface, framed in the line colour with the controls' hand-cut corners, a step down from the raised open card; on admin it is a plain block under a thin line. It reads the same for every viewer and adds no colour of its own (#103, #116).

- **The match line:** *DTDD:* in muted ink, the record's name and year as a link with ↗, then *· auto* or *· set by Ada* in muted ink.
- **Correcting the match:** the section's last row, after the matched block, the topic list and the credit, shut until asked for. Collapsed it is one quiet muted control, *Wrong film?* on any film with a match line, whatever its state, and *Match it by hand* only on one with no match at all, with no box or border. Opened, a muted line reads *Find it on DoesTheDogDie ↗, then paste its link here.*, its link in lilac, and below it sit a box as wide as the section and a quiet *Use this link*, which drops under the box on a phone. A refusal is a line of its own under the box, in the note box's dusty rose, with the pasted text kept and focused. Asked, the control shows the **preview**: *Found:* in muted ink, the record's name and year as a link with ↗, then *· Movie · 204 topics*, and under it *Use this match* in pumpkin, focused, beside a quiet *Cancel*; the match line above it stays as it was. On a film the section credits for nothing else, the preview brings the credit line under its buttons. A refusal, a preview or a pasted link opens the control, so nothing typed or found is hidden, and the rest of the section stays as it was until *Use this match* swaps the whole section in, with the new record's list; a note saved meanwhile leaves the control as it was, opened or shut (#105, #116).
- **A gap** is a muted line led by its word, *uncovered*, *not fetched* or *no match*, in a dashed muted pill, as the ledger names it, so nothing reads as a count or an all-clear. An uncovered film matched to an empty record keeps its match line above the gap.
- **The topic list** waits behind one lilac control, *All 42 topics on DoesTheDogDie*, with a chevron that turns as it opens. Each super-category is a small uppercase muted heading, and its rows are the topic in ink and *— 3 yes / 1 no* in muted tabular figures, in as many columns as the card has room for, one on a phone. It lists only topics with at least one yes vote (#103). Its rows arrive as it first opens, after a moment's muted *Fetching the topics…*, and stay open with it when a note is saved below.
- **The matched block:** for a voter with a ticked topic the film carries, above the topic list's control, a small uppercase muted heading, *On your list*, over the list's own rows, every matched topic as *dogs dying — 3 yes / 1 no*, the most yes votes first, thin or thick and with no mark of its own. It is a part of its own, `dtdd_matched.html`, which M6 places beside the veto control. A voter with nothing ticked, or with *I don't care* on, has none, and neither has the Films tab's card (#111).
- **The watch toggle:** on a voter's opened topic list, each row ends with a quiet toggle after its counts, a small lilac *Watch* with no box, edged in the line colour on hover. A row on the voter's list reads *Watching* in sea-glass, edged in the same keen green as the page's ticked box, and its toggle takes it off. Pressing one swaps the card's section in place with the list still open, so the matched block gains or loses the row and the ring appears or goes at once. It is not drawn while *I don't care* is on, nor on the Films tab's card. The gallery shows the open list with two rows watched (#114).
- **The credit**, *Powered by DoesTheDogDie.com*, ends the section a little smaller, whenever it shows a match or topics.

## My warnings

A voter page on a roomy single column. At the top sit the page's one line of purpose in a larger body size, then the privacy disclosure as a plain paragraph in muted ink, with no form chrome around either, so it reads as a few sentences and not an intake form. Under them the **chips** sit on one surface, framed in the line colour with the card corners: each ticked topic is a small pill in the score scale's *Happy* sea-glass with a *×*, and an empty list says *Nothing ticked yet.* in muted ink. Unticking a chip and unticking its row are the same post. A tick that grows or shrinks the chips box scrolls the page by the same distance, so the clicked row stays under the finger. Below the chips, one **search box** in Pico's own search field, full width of the column, with the placeholder *Search the topics, such as spiders*; it shows only once its script has run. While something is typed, only the matching rows show, inside sections opened to show them, and the other sections are gone; a line in muted ink, *No topic matches that.*, stands where the sections were when nothing matches. Clearing the box puts every section back as it was (#108). Below, each super-category is a collapsed surface of the same frame: a ▸ that turns as it opens, the name in bold, and at the end *12 topics · 2 ticked* in muted ink, the ticked part only once a topic is ticked. Opened, a dashed line starts a grid of rows, as many columns as fit, each a quiet button: a small hand-cut checkbox that fills sea-glass when ticked, then the label in ink; the description is its hover title and nothing more. Under each label, aligned with it, a small line names the films carrying the topic in ink, joined by *·*, all of them and wrapping with the row; a topic no film carries says *no films in the pool*, or *no films on the ballot* from VOTE on, in muted ink (#109). The page ends with the *Powered by DoesTheDogDie.com* link in small type. When DoesTheDogDie hasn't answered, the sections and chips give way to one line in a dashed frame saying the topics can't be shown yet. *My warnings* is the first item of the voter's name menu, above *Change password*, in every phase but RUN (#116). The page adds no colour of its own (#107).

**I don't care** sits beside the privacy disclosure, to its right and under it on a phone, as one quiet outlined pill, the same as *Withdraw…*, reversible and unremarkable. Pressed, it reads *Warn me again*, so it plainly reverses, and fills sea-glass as a ticked box does, and a muted line under it says nothing is ticked and the list can't be until it is turned off. Over ticked topics a click arms it as withdraw is armed: *Cancel* takes the pill's place at its size, and below it a bold line reads *This clears your 4 topics.* over a button naming the act, *Clear my 4 topics*. Confirmed or turned on, the list greys out: the chips and the sections drop to under half their ink, the rows' boxes can't be pressed and show a not-allowed cursor, and the search still finds them (#110, #116).

**The nag bar** is a slim band under the voter header on every voter page but *My warnings* and the forced password change, on the plain surface with a line below it, one line of small muted type centred, *You haven't set up your warnings yet.* and a bold lime *Set up my warnings* link, a trigger-warning word like the others (#117). It has no alarm red and no close, since ticking a topic or pressing *I don't care* removes it. On a phone the sentence wraps, and the link never breaks across lines (#110).

## The Films tab

A line gives the ballot's size and screen time, then the four filters sit as small square-cornered links, the chosen one edged in pumpkin, each with its count in muted ink. The ledger's approval column is a small uppercase tag: *undecided* in pumpkin, *approved* in sea-glass, *dropped* in muted ink. A title is a bold plain button that opens the card in a row of its own beneath, one at a time; the card's own title closes it again. On a phone the directors give way, the ledger scrolls sideways within its own frame, and an opened card holds still at the frame's width (#95). Its columns keep fixed widths, so every filter lays out alike and only the film's column gives, and each row's poster is a thumbnail 1.5rem wide, small enough to keep the row dense and big enough to know the film. The row under the pointer, or holding keyboard focus, lifts to the raised surface, its opened card with it, so *Approve* and *Drop* are plainly on that film (the dev, 2026-10-24). The *DTDD* column, between the runtime and the approval, gives a matched film's topic count in ink, and names each gap, *uncovered*, *not fetched* or *no match*, in muted italic, so no gap reads as a count; one quiet *Powered by DoesTheDogDie.com* link sits under the ledger (#102). Under the ballot's line, the stale line names the films whose trigger data is old or missing in muted ink, a size down, and is gone when none is (#104).

## Motion

Motion is slow and small, and it is never on the text.

- **Mist:** a lilac and ember mist drifts behind every voter and RUN page over 90 s.
- **Bat:** it roosts by the wordmark, wings spread and still, and takes off at random, 12 to 30 s apart, as `components/bat.js` picks; on admin pages it only roosts. Each flight is the theme's one jump scare: a jolt off the roost that swells as if at you, then a wide loop out to the right and home over 2.6 s, its wings flapping all the way (#99).
- **Spider:** it hangs on its silk from the bottom edge of the open **film card**'s poster, poster or title card, and bounces: a drop, a spring back up the silk, a few smaller bounces, then a while hanging still. Only the silk's length moves, so its top never leaves the poster's edge (#99).
- **Hover:** collapsed cards tilt, and buttons and score pills wobble.
- **RUN:** the now-playing box breathes like a candle over 4.3 s.
- **Reduced motion:** under `prefers-reduced-motion` everything stands still.
- **Admin:** nothing moves on admin. The console's wordmark keeps its bat, roosting, and no admin card has a spider.

## Adding a colour

A new colour must stay distinct from every state colour above and from the pumpkin, plum and lilac, so that no state reads as the accent. Body text keeps WCAG AA against its ground; soften with warm off-blacks and off-whites, never with dim text. Ink measures 13.4:1 on the ground and muted ink 6.7:1 on raised. Texture and mist sit behind text, never on it.
